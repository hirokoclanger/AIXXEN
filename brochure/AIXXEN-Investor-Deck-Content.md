### Slide 6 / Why now
Headline: Why now.
Body: AI spend is compounding faster than any software category before it. IDC
puts enterprise AI spend at $307B in 2025, rising toward $632B by 2028. The money
is being committed today. The tooling to measure its return has not kept pace.
Whoever builds that measurement in early compounds an advantage on every budget
cycle that follows. AIXXEN is built for that window.
Source: IDC, Worldwide AI and Generative AI Spending Guide, 2025.

### Slide 7 / Market size
Headline: The market.

Three nested figures:

- TAM, about EUR 2.8B. Enterprise AI spend reaches USD 307B in 2025 (IDC).
  AIXXEN's market is the software budget to measure and steer that spend, sized
  at roughly 1% of it, the rate management software typically captures of the
  spend it governs.
- SAM, about EUR 1.0B. The mid-market and enterprise segment AIXXEN serves in
  the EU and US, near one third of TAM. At a EUR 65k licence that is roughly
  15,000 addressable companies.
- SOM, about EUR 32M ARR. A five-year obtainable target of around 500 customers
  at EUR 65k, close to 3% of SAM. The nearer three-year mark is about EUR 6.5M
  ARR at 100 customers.

Source for the backdrop: IDC, Worldwide AI and Generative AI Spending Guide,
2025. The 1% capture rate and the one-third SAM share are stated model
assumptions, not published figures.

### Slide 8 / Competitors
Headline: Who else is in the room.
Body: Three categories of tool touch AI spend. None of them measure what it
returns.

- Spend and FinOps tools. Snow Software (now Flexera), Apptio, Zylo, SAP Ariba,
  Microsoft Cost Management. They track what software and cloud cost. The
  benefit side stays blank.
- AI governance platforms. Microsoft Purview, IBM watsonx.governance, Credo AI,
  Holistic AI. They manage AI for risk, policy and model inventory. They answer
  whether AI is allowed to run. They do not answer whether it pays.
- Usage and BI dashboards. Microsoft Copilot adoption analytics, Glean, and
  general BI such as Power BI and Tableau. They show adoption and activity.
  Activity is not a P&L number.

AIXXEN connects all three. What AI costs, what it returns per use case, and
whether the workforce can realize that return. That is the gap on this slide.

### Slide 10 / Competitive positioning map
*(template logo-scatter slide)*

Two independent axes, so every category lands somewhere real:

- X axis, Spend visibility. Low on the left, high on the right. Does the tool
  show what AI costs?
- Y axis, Return visibility. Low at the bottom, high at the top. Does the tool
  show what AI delivers?

Where each logo sits:

- Spend and FinOps tools (Apptio, Zylo, Flexera, SAP Ariba): bottom right. Strong
  on cost, blank on return.
- AI governance platforms (Microsoft Purview, Credo AI, Holistic AI): bottom
  left. They map risk and policy, not cost and not return.
- Usage and BI dashboards (Copilot adoption analytics, Glean, Power BI): lower
  middle. They show activity, which is neither cost nor return.
- AIXXEN: top right, alone. The only tool that shows both.

Speaker note, not on the slide: the top-left quadrant is empty because nothing
can show return without first tracking cost. That absence is the point. Plenty
of tools see the cost. None of them see the return.
