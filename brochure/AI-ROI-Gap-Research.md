# The AI Adoption-to-ROI Gap — Research Notes

Source-attributed research compiled for the AIXXEN business brochure. Every figure
below is tied to a named organization, report and year. Figures flagged with a
caution should be checked against the primary source before being quoted verbatim.

## 1. Source-attributed data points

1. **Enterprise AI spending reaches $307 billion in 2025**, rising to $632 billion by 2028.
   IDC, *Worldwide AI and Generative AI Spending Guide*, 2025.

2. **AI spending grows roughly 31.9% per year (2025–2029), reaching $1.3 trillion by 2029.**
   IDC, *Worldwide AI and Generative AI Spending Guide*, 2025.

3. **88% of organizations report regular AI use in at least one business function**, up from 78% a year earlier.
   McKinsey, *The State of AI in 2025: Agents, Innovation, and Transformation*, 2025.

4. **Only about 6% of organizations qualify as "AI high performers"** — attributing 5% or more of EBIT to AI.
   McKinsey, *The State of AI in 2025*, 2025.

5. **39% of organizations report any enterprise-level EBIT impact from AI**, and most of those put it under 5% of EBIT.
   McKinsey, *The State of AI in 2025*, 2025. *(Caution: the exact "39%" wording varies across secondary summaries; verify against the McKinsey PDF before quoting.)*

6. **95% of enterprise generative-AI pilots deliver no measurable P&L impact**; only about 5% achieve rapid revenue acceleration, despite $30–40 billion in enterprise GenAI spending.
   MIT NANDA, *The GenAI Divide: State of AI in Business 2025*, 2025.

7. **Only 5% of companies are achieving AI value at scale**; 60% are "laggards" seeing minimal revenue or cost gains.
   BCG, *The Widening AI Value Gap: Build for the Future 2025*, September 2025.

8. **AI "future-built" companies achieve 5x the revenue gains and 3x the cost reductions** of other firms.
   BCG, *The Widening AI Value Gap*, 2025.

9. **At least 30% of generative-AI projects will be abandoned after proof of concept by end-2025**, citing poor data quality, weak risk controls, escalating costs or unclear business value.
   Gartner, press release, July 2024.

10. **Over 40% of agentic-AI projects will be cancelled by end of 2027.**
    Gartner, press release, June 2025.

11. **Only 28% of AI use cases in infrastructure and operations fully succeed and meet ROI expectations; 20% fail outright.**
    Gartner, 2025–2026.

12. **85% of organizations increased AI investment last year and 91% plan to increase it again — yet only 6% saw payback within one year**; typical AI ROI takes 2–4 years.
    Deloitte, *AI ROI: The Paradox of Rising Investment and Elusive Returns*, 2025 (survey of 1,854 executives).

13. **Only about 1 in 5 organizations qualify as "AI ROI Leaders"** — those that measure success rigorously and treat AI as organizational transformation.
    Deloitte, *AI ROI: The Paradox* / *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise*, 2025.

14. **69% of respondents say fully implementing an AI governance strategy will take more than a year**, and only 1 in 5 companies has a mature governance model for autonomous AI agents.
    Deloitte, *State of Generative AI in the Enterprise*, 2025.

15. **Only 37% of organizations have policies to manage or detect shadow AI**; breaches involving high shadow AI add roughly $670,000 to the average breach cost.
    IBM, *Cost of a Data Breach Report 2025*.

16. **Spending on AI-native applications rose 108% in 2025 (average $1.2M per organization)**; only about 40% of companies had purchased official AI subscriptions despite 665 distinct GenAI tools found in enterprise environments.
    Harmonic Security analysis, 2025 (cited via Fortune). *(Caution: vendor research, not a Big-Four or analyst source. Prefer IBM's Cost of a Data Breach 2025 as the primary shadow-AI citation.)*

## 2. The "gap" narrative

The evidence converges on one credible story. **Enterprise AI adoption has raced ahead
of AI accountability.** Nearly nine in ten enterprises now use AI and spending is
compounding past 30% a year, yet only a tiny minority — 5 to 6%, on both BCG's and
McKinsey's numbers — can show real enterprise-level financial return. Three different
methodologies land in the same place: McKinsey's EBIT lens, BCG's value-at-scale
segmentation and MIT NANDA's pilot-level P&L analysis all produce a single-digit
success rate.

The reason is not the technology. It is the absence of measurement, governance and
workflow discipline. Pilots stall (Gartner: 30%+ abandoned), ROI goes untracked
(Deloitte: only about 1 in 5 are "ROI Leaders"; only 6% see payback within a year),
governance lags (only 37% have shadow-AI policies), and spend fragments into
ungoverned shadow AI. AIXXEN sits in this gap. It converts diffuse, unmeasured AI
spend into a tracked portfolio with use-case-level ROI, a hard-versus-soft savings
split, and governance visibility — the discipline the research repeatedly names as
the missing ingredient.
