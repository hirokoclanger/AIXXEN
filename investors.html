The wedge

The vendors won the AI race. The leverage moved to the workforce.

Existing tooling treats AI as a procurement category, a compliance question, or a generic SaaS line item. None measure the gap that actually drives ROI.

What we observe

The AI itself is no longer the bottleneck. OpenAI, Anthropic, Microsoft, and Google ship cheaper, faster, more capable models every quarter. The same tool deployed in two organizations produces wildly different outcomeshelps enterprises and the difference is rarely the model.

The AI ROI conversation has moved entirely to the workforce side: how well employees have been trained to use the tools, motivated to adopt them, and given the time to build new habits. Until you can measure that, every soft-saving projection is a vendor pitch.

Capability is the missing primitive. Every adjacent tool in EA, FinOps, governance, and model risk assumes the value is in the AI. AIXXEN inverts that. The product is the workforce; the AI is a tool. We measure both sides, on one page.

What that produces

A defensible CFO conversation. Hard P&L savings (booked, attested) sit in a different column from soft potential (capacity freed). Soft potential is multiplied by an organization-specific capability factor before it lands anywhere near a board pack.

A targeted CIO conversation. Capability scores per department surface where training and enablement spend will produce the biggest delta. Shadow AI share surfaces the spend that's happening outside procurement.

Defensibility lives in the measurement methodology. The capability factor is calibrated by a proprietary anonymous instrument that produces tenant-specific numbershelps enterprises not vendor benchmarks. The methodology is the moat; details under NDA.
